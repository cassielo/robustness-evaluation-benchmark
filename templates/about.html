About Our Benchmark

Why we need a benchmark?

Recently developed adversarial attacks on neural networks have become more aggressive and dangerous, because of which Artificial Intelligence (AI) models are no longer sufficiently robust against them. It is important to have a set of effective and reliable methods to detect malicious attacks to ensure the security of AI models. Such standardized methods can also serve as a reference for researchers to develop robust models and new kinds of attacks.

The algorithm is applicable to any neural network model. Under our observation, there is no AI model that can defend every attack. An AI model may have good defensive capabilities against some attacks but is readily susceptible to others.

We proposed a robustness evaluation algorithm that measures a variety of attacks on the AI models to improve their defense. Regardless of the numbers of attacks or models used, our algorithm can validate the robustness of the model in an unbiased way.

What is adversarial attack?

For image recognition models trained with ImageNet or other datasets, often small changes added in the input can make the output change significantly. There are very small changes to the image and it looks no difference to the naked eye.

When training AI model, the input will be a clean and noise-free image and the output will be the category of the image. After knowing which category the image is, we use adversarial attack to add some noise that the naked eye cannot detect. Then, put the adversarial example as input again for the AI model to get the classification output.

The adversarial attack is effective when the model classifies the adversarial example as different category from the original image.

How to evaluate the robustness ?

Our system uses a scoring algorithm to compute the stability of models and is not limited to the attack type. With more different types of attacks, the robustness evaluation is more complete.

The Robustness Evaluate Benchmark is a platform for researchers to test the robustness of their models or the effectiveness of adversarial attacks. The goal is to use this co-evolution to find truly robust models that resist even the most effective adversarial attacks.

We test with three independent datasets, one for each of the common datasets and tasks: MNIST, CIFAR, and ImageNet.

Datasets

We use three independent datasets

MNIST

MNIST requires the robust classification of handwritten digits with a resolution of 28x28 pixels.

CIFAR-10

CIFAR-10 requires the robust classification of 32x32 pixel natural images from ten different classes.

ImageNet

ImageNet requires the robust classification of 256x256 pixel natural images into 1000 classes.
